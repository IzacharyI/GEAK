## What this is, and what it is not

This is the **evidence layer**, not the final development advice: an index of the same operator
family written six ways. It lets a FlyDSL author inspect how somebody else answered a question
before answering it from scratch. Every row carries a content-bound `src_…` identity and
`file:line` in an AITER checkout at a named commit.

It contains **no performance claims and no rankings.** Nothing here says a tile is good, or
that one language beats another. A source statement is mechanically reproducible; a recommendation
needs conditions and evidence strength. Those actionable cards live in [`gemm_decisions.md`](gemm_decisions.md). Measured claims are not copied into that always-on page; they remain behind the learned-card/expert-skill switches with their validation artifact.

Read a count as **"this many source locations matched this implementation question"** — never as
"this language does this and the others do not." A compiler-chosen answer still exists; it
is simply not in the source text. See the coverage note below.

The zeros are the interesting part, and they are easy to misread. `scheduling` is dense in
FlyDSL and empty elsewhere, but Triton kernels are scheduled too — by a compiler pass, and CK
by a `BlockGemmPipelineScheduler` enum, neither of which is a per-instruction statement a grep
can find. Likewise `tile_shape` is nearly empty for Triton not because Triton kernels have no
tiles, but because their tiles arrive as `tl.constexpr` arguments whose values are in the
shipped JSON (see the tunable-surface and tuned-starting-point sections).

So: a filled cell means *an answer is written here, at these lines*. An empty cell means
*not stated in source in a form this extractor recognises* — which may mean the answer is
delegated, encoded in a name, or simply that no rule exists for that idiom yet. The rules are
listed in `_extract_impl_facts.py`; a missing idiom is a fixable gap, not a finding.
